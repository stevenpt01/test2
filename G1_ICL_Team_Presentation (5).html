The problem we have today

Collect

Every new task variant means a fresh teleoperation session on the robot.

Retrain

A fine-tuning run, checkpoint selection and a new deployment bundle.

Redeploy

Goal: replace that cycle with one demonstration in the prompt — no weight updates between task variants.

Presenter notesC is the context: one or more demonstrations, optionally language or a goal image. H is the live observation history. Training beforehand is allowed and usually essential; the claim is only that θ is fixed during every test rollout. 01 · ICL and the state of the art

Two ways context can teach a robot: LocoFormer and S1

LocoFormer · context = the robot's own experience

A single locomotion policy for unseen legged and wheeled robots — including a G1 — with no precise kinematics given. Transformer-XL keeps memory across trials: it learns from falls in early trials and walks in later ones. Trained by massive RL on procedurally generated robots with aggressive domain randomization. CoRL 2025 best-paper finalist.

↔

S1 · context = one video of a person

Skild's manipulation foundation model, built from the ground up as an in-context learner. One egocentric human video → the robot executes unseen tasks up to 10 minutes long: plant potting, pancakes, pour-over coffee, kit assembly. Same weights for every example; no post-training.

Same company, same idea, two paradigms from our four-way map: LocoFormer is in-context RL (row 3); S1 is demonstration-prompted from human video (row 1). Neither ships weights — they are the clearest available explanation of what ICL is, not something we can run.
Presenter notesUse these as the teaching pair. LocoFormer: the "demo" is the robot's own history; adaptation is rebuilding an embodiment representation online, and the G1 push and walking videos on the project page show it on our robot class. S1: the demo is a person doing the task; the policy must infer intent, functional correspondences and task progress across scene, viewpoint and embodiment. Skild frames pre-training as the outer loop that teaches learning-from-context, and the prompt as the inner loop with no weight change — exactly the definition on slide 3. Timeline: LocoFormer Sept 2025 → first in-domain ICL Feb 2026 → first pancake May 2026 → S1 Aug 2026.

01 · ICL and the state of the art

GEN-1.5: in-context learning that was never trained for

  1. Pretrain on raw experienceLarge multimodal model, 30 s context, 100 Hz actions; >8 months of continuous pretraining on ~1.9M scenes from homes, warehouses and factories. No meta-learning loop, no ICL architecture, no auxiliary objectives.
  2. Physical promptInsert a 3–12 s sensorimotor clip (sensors + actions, recorded with handheld grippers or by the robot) at the front of the context; the rest holds rolling observations.
  3. Execute with zero gradient steps59% ± 10% average success across 10 unseen short-horizon tasks (zippers, jar lids, wallets).
  4. Prompt engineeringCompose demo A + demo B into one behaviour with invented bridging motions; prompt from simulation with no sim data in pretraining; a person's own hands in view can be the prompt.
  5. Optional few-step adaptation1–10 gradient steps on 1–5 min of data → 83% ± 9%; ten steps move weights by <0.15%. One step on one minute: 66.5%.
Lesson: past a pretraining threshold, adaptation cost collapses and one-shot ICL and one-step fine-tuning become two faces of "reminding the model of something it nearly knows." Closed model, no weights — a concept source, not a candidate.
Presenter notesContrast with everything open we can run: ICRT, BPP, MimicDroid and Instant Policy all train for ICL with a support/query split. GEN-1.5 did not, and prompts introduce time jumps it never saw, yet it works. Their hypotheses are the LLM ones: burstiness and Zipfian structure of physical data, and repetitive cycles the model learns to extend. Three ideas transfer to our plan regardless of model: a library of short composable prompts instead of one long demo; sim-recorded prompts as a cheap prompt source; and an evaluation row comparing one-shot in context against 1–5 gradient steps on the same data.

FACTR 2: not ICL, but two tools our G1 plan is missing

NEXT · force sensing without a sensor

Collect ~10 min of free-space motion; train an LSTM (≈1 min) to predict measured joint torque (current × torque constant) from joint state. Deploy: external torque = measured − predicted. Near zero in free motion, matches Franka's factory torque sensors in contact; enables force-feedback teleop on Piper and YAM arms.

+

FIRST · force-informed re-sampling

Run NEXT over every demo, segment into free-space / pre-contact / contact, and up-sample the contact-relevant samples in each BC batch. Across five contact-rich long-horizon tasks (NIST belt, cap screwing, insertion, tool clean-up, LEGO): >17% higher task progress than prior force-aware policies, more recovery, better robustness.

For the G1 (no F/T sensors, joint current available): a real contact signal for the cup verifier, phase labels for teleop data QA and FIRST-style training of the GR00T baseline or BPP, and haptics for the operator — all inside week 1. Code is public.
Presenter notesWhy it is in an ICL deck: Skild cites this paper for the finding that dense from-scratch post-training matches a post-trained foundation model, i.e. the purpose of pretraining is ICL, not better fine-tuning. FACTR 2 itself is a better way to do behaviour cloning. Grasp verification today is "gripper closed and TCP rising"; with NEXT it becomes "closed, rising, and carrying an external load", which is a stronger signal for the verifier and for the GRASP_MISS / SLIP failure codes.

BPP + iPhUMI on the G1, step by step

  1. Record promptOne handheld iPhUMI demo of cup → coaster, no robot: wrist RGB 60 Hz, ARKit 6-DoF pose, gripper width via finger tags. One-time width calibration.
  2. Build promptConvert to sensorimotor data (wrist frame, EE pose + width, relative-EE actions). Downsample obs/proprio to ~1 Hz, keep actions at full rate, attention-pool each chunk → P = [p₀…pₙ]; cache once per rollout.
  3. Match the eyesiPhone (or camera at the iPhUMI offset) on the G1 parallel gripper, streamed as the wrist camera; gripper width calibrated to the same scalar.
  4. InferPrompt encoder cross-attends the current observation to P; diffusion U-Net decodes a chunk of [Δpose, width] relative to the current EE pose. Gateway does IK, reach and collision checks, executes a short prefix.
  5. Swap promptRecord bowl → mug with iPhUMI; same weights, same scene, different P → different behaviour. This is the two-cup test.
  6. If neededTrain on our iPhUMI data: many tasks × few demos, split by task; one demo as prompt, other demos of the same task as queries.
Released: MIT code, checkpoints, deployment stack and the iPhUMI app. Known limit: weak conditioning under low training diversity, and no new action primitive from a single prompt.
Presenter notesWalk this the way the KAT example was walked: input on the left, output on the right. Numbers to quote: prompt encoder ~57M params, action decoder ~151M, real drawing data was 200 human tasks × 5 demos plus 800 scripted tasks. The transfer risk for us is the action space: UMI-style relative end-effector trajectories and a matched wrist-camera pose. If those two are reproduced on the G1 gripper, a released checkpoint is worth trying before any training; if not, ICRT is the fallback. Paper Sections 3.3–3.4 and Appendix I.
